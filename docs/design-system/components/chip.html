Chip

Chips are small rounded controls: filters over a list, quick replies to the agent, and the mode chip that says whether the agent or the checklist is running.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable

Overview

  • Filter chip (.chip with aria-pressed): narrows saved devices or the evidence log; several can be on.
  • Quick reply (.reply): a one-tap answer to the agent’s question, such as Treadmill or 0x1826. Each sends its text as the person’s message.
  • Mode chip (.mode-chip): static, in the app bar, “Agent” or “Checklist” (FR-005).

Use a checkbox for a form choice and a button for anything that is not a short answer or a filter.

Anatomy

  1. LabelOne or two words.
  2. Container48px tall pill; filled ink when pressed.

Variants

Filter chips scroll sideways on phones.

Responsive behaviour

  • Filter chips stay on one line and scroll horizontally with the page margin as padding; they never wrap to two rows.
  • Replies wrap onto as many rows as they need, 8px apart.

Theming

Pressed filter chips use --color-accent with --color-fg-on-accent, the same pair as the primary button. Replies sit on --color-bg-canvas inside the surface-coloured dock so they read as separate tappable shapes.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Filter chips: <button aria-pressed> inside a labelled group. Replies: plain buttons inside .replies. Mode chip: text.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabMoves through chips one by one.
Enter, SpaceToggles a filter or sends a reply.

Focus

The shared ring (--focus-ring-width 2px of --color-focus-ring, 2px offset) appears on :focus-visible only, so a tap never leaves a ring behind.

Labelling

The chip text is the name; pressed state is announced from aria-pressed. Group filters in role="group" with aria-label="Filter saved devices".

Motion

Content

  • Filter labels are the verdict words: Connected, Partial, Needs a tool, Not connectable.
  • Replies are what the person would say, short and literal; the agent receives the text.
